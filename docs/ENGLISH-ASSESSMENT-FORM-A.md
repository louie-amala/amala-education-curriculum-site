# Part 2B — Reach booklet

_20 minutes._

> Nur Begum is twenty years old and lives in Camp 12. Two years ago she joined an English class at
> her learning centre. She enjoyed it, but she noticed that many of her friends stopped coming after
> a few weeks. When she asked them why, most said the same thing: they were too shy to speak in
> front of a big group, and they were afraid of making mistakes.
>
> Nur decided to try something small. Every Friday afternoon, she invited four of her neighbours to
> sit together outside her shelter and practise English for one hour. There was no teacher and no
> book. They simply asked each other simple questions and helped each other find words.
>
> At first the group was quiet. After a month, though, the four women were talking easily, and two
> of them went back to the class at the centre. Others heard about the group and asked to join, so
> Nur now runs two groups instead of one.
>
> She says the most important thing is not the English. It is that nobody in the group laughs when
> someone makes a mistake.

# Part 3 — Oral check

_One to one. 6 minutes. **Optional, and the last thing to drop.** If there is not time for everyone,
assess a random sample of at least 10 learners or 40% of the class, chosen before the baseline, and
assess the same learners at endline._

_Same-gender pairing where it matters. The learner may pass on anything.
Nothing is recorded unless the learner agrees._

| | What you say | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Warm-up** _(not scored)_ | "Hello. My name is ___. What is your name?" | 30s |
| **2. Personal information** | Ask, wait, and ask once more in simpler words if needed: your name · your age, or how long you have been here · who you live with. **Do not ask where they are from.** | 90s |
| **3. Picture** | Show the Form A picture — people outside a learning centre. "What can you see? Tell me about this picture." | 90s |
