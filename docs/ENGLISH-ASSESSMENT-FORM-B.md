> from an older neighbour. He was good at it, but he had a problem: he owned only one set of tools,

>
> At first they argued about whose turn it was. Then Ayub suggested something simple. They wrote the
> days of the week on a piece of card and hung it outside his shelter. Each person took two days,

> use them.

## Three things the check surfaced

1. **F3 is a seventh anchor, unavoidably.** The component puts only the learner's name and the three
   frames in front of learners in print, so both forms have to test the same five words. Only the
   order differs. Treat F3 as an anchor when reading drift, not as an independent measure.
2. **F4's item pool is nearly exhausted.** With s·t·l·m·n·a·o·u·c taught and the plosives excluded,
   there are about eight decodable, picturable words in the whole taught set. Form A takes four
   (*sun · man · sock · nut*), Form B takes the other four (*cat · mat · cot · can*). There is no
   third set. A Form C would have to repeat.
3. **Form B's L13 changes the reason, not the task.** Form A's note says why you cannot come to
   class; Form B's says what happened in the class your friend missed. Both are a ~40-word note with
   three given content points. The topic domain is identical; only the situation moves — which is
   the rule.
